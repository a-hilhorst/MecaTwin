# MecaTwin

**Analysis of mechanical twinning in single-phase FCC alloys**

## Overview

This repository provides the data and analysis scripts supporting the scientific manuscript:

> **“On the relationship between the stacking fault energy of FCC metallic alloys and their mechanical properties”**

The repository is made publicly available in accordance with current best practices for open and reproducible science. It contains the complete database developed in the study, along with the scripts used for data processing, analysis, and figure generation.
The primary objective of this repository is to enable transparency, reproducibility, and reuse of the data and methodologies employed in the investigation of deformation mechanisms in face-centered cubic (FCC) metallic alloys.

## Scientific Context

Deformation twinning is a key plasticity mechanism in face-centered cubic (FCC) metals and alloys and is often associated with enhanced strain hardening and ductility. Despite its recognized benefits, incomplete understanding of the parameters governing twinning activation limits the rational design of advanced alloys. In this work, the role of stacking fault energy (SFE) in controlling deformation mechanisms and mechanical properties is systematically assessed. A comprehensive database comprising more than one thousand entries is constructed, incorporating experimentally measured SFE values and tensile properties, including a subset of alloys for which both quantities are available, to examine relationships among chemical composition, SFE, activated deformation mechanisms, and strain hardening behavior. The analysis reveals that thermodynamic-based SFE models are highly sensitive to Gibbs energy descriptions and interface energy parameters, which limits their applicability for alloy design across broad composition ranges. While SFE remains a relevant indicator for predicting the activation of the twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) mechanism, it is not a sufficient descriptor on its own, as other material parameters play comparably important roles. Alloys exhibiting TWIP generally possess higher SFE values than those showing transformation-induced plasticity via epsilon-martensite, whereas SFE has a limited influence on FCC-to-BCC transformations. Both TWIP and TRIP mechanisms enhance strain hardening, with no single mechanism being intrinsically superior in alloys with low interstitial content. In carbon-containing alloys, strain hardening is governed by a coupled effect of carbon concentration and SFE, with higher carbon content and lower SFE promoting enhanced hardening.
